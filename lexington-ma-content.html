About Lexington, MA

Lexington, Massachusetts holds a singular place in American history as the site of the first battles of the Revolutionary War on April 19, 1775. Today, this storied community blends its rich heritage with an exceptional quality of life that consistently ranks it among the best places to live in New England. Located just 12 miles northwest of Boston along Route 2, Lexington is the premier destination for families seeking outstanding public schools, a tight-knit community, and easy access to Greater Boston's world-class employment centers.

What truly sets Lexington apart is the extraordinary caliber of its public school system. Families from across the region — and the world — relocate specifically to Lexington for its schools, which consistently rank among the very best in Massachusetts. When my clients say they want a great school district, Lexington is always at the top of the list. The town's commitment to educational excellence is reflected in its investment in facilities, curriculum, and teacher quality.

Beyond education, Lexington offers a quintessential New England lifestyle. The historic Battle Green sits at the heart of Lexington Center, surrounded by independent shops, acclaimed restaurants, and the beautifully preserved Minuteman National Historical Park. Residents enjoy 1,700+ acres of conservation land, the beloved Minuteman Bikeway, and a genuine sense of community cultivated through town meeting governance, local sports leagues, and vibrant cultural programming. If you're looking for the best of suburban Massachusetts living, Lexington delivers on every front.

Schools & Education in Lexington

Lexington Public Schools are widely recognized as one of the premier school systems in Massachusetts, earning an A+ rating from Niche.com. For families prioritizing top-ranked public schools, Lexington is consistently the gold standard in Greater Boston.

When clients ask me, "Where should I buy if schools are my top priority?" — Lexington is my first answer. The district invests heavily in STEM programs, arts, athletics, and student support services. Graduates regularly gain admission to the nation's most selective universities. This is the school system families relocate to Massachusetts for.

Frequently Asked Questions About Living in Lexington, MA

Are Lexington schools really as good as people say?

Yes — and the numbers back it up. Lexington Public Schools earn an A+ rating from Niche.com and are consistently ranked among the top 5-10 public school districts in all of Massachusetts. Lexington High School regularly appears on lists of the best public high schools in the country. The district boasts exceptional SAT/ACT scores, a high percentage of students taking AP courses, and outstanding college placement results including Ivy League and top technical institutions. If public school quality is your primary criterion for choosing a town, Lexington belongs at the top of your list.

What is the typical home price range in Lexington?

Lexington is a premium market. Single-family homes typically range from approximately $1,000,000 for smaller or older homes needing updates, to $2,500,000+ for newer construction and larger properties. The median single-family home price is around $1,750,000. Condominiums and townhomes (less common) can start around $600,000. Given the school district premium, Lexington commands significantly higher prices than surrounding towns. Buyers should be prepared for competitive, often multiple-offer situations, particularly for well-priced homes.

How is the commute from Lexington to Boston?

By car via Route 2, the commute to downtown Boston or Cambridge takes about 25-35 minutes off-peak and 45-60 minutes during peak rush hours. Many Lexington residents drive to Alewife Station (Red Line, ~20-25 min) or take the MBTA Route 76 bus to Alewife, then ride the subway into Boston, Cambridge, or MIT/Harvard. The total commute by transit is typically 50-70 minutes. The Minuteman Bikeway is also popular for cycling to Alewife for transit. Overall, Lexington is very well connected to Greater Boston's major employment hubs including the Route 128 tech/biotech corridor right on the town's doorstep.

Is Lexington a good place for families with young children?

Lexington is frequently cited as one of the best communities in New England for raising a family. The combination of outstanding public schools (K-12), abundant parks and conservation land, the Minuteman Bikeway for safe cycling, active youth sports leagues, a tight-knit community culture, and low crime rates makes it exceptional. The town's walkable historic center, community events like Patriots' Day, and strong sense of civic pride add to the family-friendly atmosphere. Many of my clients with young children specifically choose Lexington as their forever home.

What are property taxes like in Lexington?

Lexington's property tax rate is approximately $13-15 per $1,000 of assessed value (confirm the current rate with the Lexington Assessor's Office). On a home assessed at $1,500,000, you'd expect annual property taxes around $20,000-$22,500. While not the lowest in the region, Lexington's taxes directly fund its exemplary school system and public services — a tradeoff most residents consider well worth it. Prospective buyers should factor property taxes into their budget calculations; I'm happy to help you estimate taxes for any specific property.

How competitive is the Lexington real estate market?

Very competitive. Lexington consistently operates in a seller's market due to high demand from school-district-focused buyers and relatively limited inventory. Well-priced, move-in-ready homes routinely receive multiple offers within days of listing, often selling above asking price (the average sale-to-list ratio exceeds 100%). Buyers need to be pre-approved, financially strong, and ready to act decisively. Working with a buyer's agent who knows the Lexington market — and has experience winning in competitive situations — is essential. That's exactly what I offer.

What architectural styles of homes are common in Lexington?

Lexington's housing stock reflects its long history and suburban growth. You'll find authentic Colonial and Cape Cod homes from the 18th and 19th centuries, mid-century Ranches and Split-Levels from the 1950s-70s, and newer construction Colonials and Contemporaries from the 1990s through today. Many buyers prefer updating older homes for character and value, while others seek newer builds for modern finishes and systems. Lexington also has several townhome and condo developments, though single-family homes dominate the market.

How does Lexington compare to neighboring Belmont and Brookline for schools?

All three — Lexington, Belmont, and Brookline — offer excellent public school systems and are among the top choices for school-focused buyers in Greater Boston. Lexington is generally considered the frontrunner for school quality, consistently ranked #1 or #2 statewide. Belmont offers a slightly lower price point with still-excellent schools (rated A by Niche). Brookline's schools are outstanding (A+ from Niche) and benefit from an urban-suburban setting closer to Boston. Your best choice depends on commute, budget, and lifestyle preferences. I'd love to help you compare all three based on your specific needs.
