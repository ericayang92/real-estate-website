About Cambridge, MA

Cambridge is a thriving city across the Charles River from Boston, renowned for its world-class universities, innovative tech scene, and rich cultural diversity. Home to Harvard University and MIT, Cambridge attracts brilliant minds from around the globe, creating a dynamic community that values education, innovation, and progressive thinking. The city offers an exceptional blend of historic charm and modern sophistication, with tree-lined streets of Victorian homes, bustling squares filled with independent shops and restaurants, and cutting-edge research facilities driving the future of technology and medicine.

Living in Cambridge means enjoying world-class dining, vibrant arts venues, beautiful riverside parks, and excellent schools—all while maintaining easy access to downtown Boston. Whether you're drawn to the intellectual energy of Harvard Square, the tech innovation of Kendall Square, or the residential charm of neighborhoods like Huron Village and Cambridgeport, Cambridge offers diverse options for homebuyers seeking an engaged, walkable community with unparalleled amenities.

Cambridge, MA - Frequently Asked Questions

Is Cambridge, MA a good place to live?

Yes! Cambridge consistently ranks as one of the best places to live in Massachusetts. It offers world-class universities, innovative job opportunities (especially in tech and biotech), excellent schools, diverse dining and culture, beautiful parks, and easy access to Boston. The community is progressive, educated, and welcoming.

What is the cost of living in Cambridge, MA?

Cambridge has a higher cost of living compared to the national average, primarily due to housing costs. The median home price is around $1.25 million. However, many residents find the investment worthwhile given the exceptional schools, job opportunities, transit access, and quality of life. Renters can expect to pay $2,500-$4,000+ for a 2-bedroom apartment.

What are the best neighborhoods in Cambridge for families?

Popular family-friendly neighborhoods include Huron Village (quiet, great schools), West Cambridge (spacious homes, family-oriented), Mid-Cambridge (tree-lined streets, parks), and Cambridgeport (diverse community, walkable). All Cambridge neighborhoods have access to excellent public schools and recreational facilities.

How is the commute from Cambridge to Boston?

The commute is excellent! The MBTA Red Line runs through Cambridge with six stations (Alewife, Davis, Porter, Harvard, Central, Kendall/MIT), providing 15-20 minute rides to downtown Boston. Many residents also bike or walk across the bridges. Cambridge's public transit access is one of the best in the Boston area.

Are Cambridge schools good?

Yes, Cambridge Public Schools are highly regarded. The district offers diverse educational approaches including traditional, Montessori, and innovative programs. Schools like Cambridgeport School and Tobin Montessori consistently receive high ratings. The high school (Cambridge Rindge and Latin) offers strong AP programs and college preparation. Additionally, there are excellent private school options like Buckingham Browne & Nichols.

What is Cambridge known for?

Cambridge is world-famous for Harvard University and MIT, making it a global center for education and innovation. It's known for its tech and biotech industries (Kendall Square is often called "the most innovative square mile on the planet"), vibrant cultural scene, diverse dining, progressive politics, and historic architecture. The city has produced numerous Nobel laureates, groundbreaking startups, and cultural icons.

Is Cambridge walkable?

Absolutely! Cambridge has a Walk Score of 89 (Very Walkable) and a Bike Score of 82 (Very Bikeable). Most errands can be accomplished on foot, with grocery stores, restaurants, shops, and transit all within walking distance in most neighborhoods. The city has invested heavily in bike infrastructure and is part of the Bluebikes bike-share system.

What's the difference between Cambridge and Boston?

While adjacent to each other, Cambridge and Boston are separate cities. Cambridge is generally more residential and academic, home to Harvard and MIT, with a slightly younger demographic and progressive culture. Boston is larger, more corporate, and includes the financial district and major sports venues. Cambridge offers excellent access to Boston while maintaining its own distinct community identity. Many residents appreciate Cambridge's neighborhood feel combined with big-city amenities.
